Sir/Madam,

Q6(a): Franz is a careless and lazy boy who avoids studies and prefers watching birds and playing outdoors. On the last day of French lessons he is shocked to see the villagers sitting in class and M. Hamel dressed in his best clothes. Hearing that French will no longer be taught, he regrets his negligence deeply and tries hard to understand the lesson. The change in him shows that people realise the worth of their language and culture only when it is snatched away.

Q6(b): The lesson 'Deep Water' by William Douglas conveys the message that fear can be overcome through determination and persistent effort. As a boy, Douglas was nearly drowned in a swimming pool and developed a terrible fear of water that haunted him for years. Instead of surrendering, he hired an instructor and practised patiently every day till he could swim confidently. His victory shows that with courage, will power and constant practice, even the deepest fear can be conquered.

Q7(a): The poet uses vivid imagery in 'My Mother at Sixty-Six'. The mother's pale face is compared to the winter moon, which suggests her old age and fading vitality. In contrast, the young trees seem to be sprinting and the merry children are spilling out of their homes, images of life and energy that highlight the poet's fear of losing her mother.

Q8: The Champaran episode of 1917 was a turning point in Gandhi's life and in India's freedom struggle. Responding to Rajkumar Shukla's plea, Gandhi went to Champaran in Bihar to investigate the plight of the indigo sharecroppers who were forced to grow indigo under the oppressive tinkathia system. Defying the order to leave the district, he faced trial peacefully, and the case against him was withdrawn. It was Gandhi's first satyagraha in India, and it established non-violence as a powerful weapon. The landlords agreed to refund part of the money they had extracted. The episode also taught the farmers self-reliance and made Gandhi a national leader.
